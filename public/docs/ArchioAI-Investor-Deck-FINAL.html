The Market Problem (Simple English)

Purpose

Explain the problem to someone who has never traded. Make them understand that trading today requires navigating 6+ completely disconnected platforms to complete a single workflow. This is the "hook" slide — it must be immediately clear and relatable.

Suggested Copy

Modern trading workflows are spread across too many platforms.

Traders and communities do not operate in one system. They navigate a messy patchwork of disconnected tools:

• Analysis happens in one app
• Execution happens in another
• Communication happens elsewhere
• Account management happens on a separate website
• Journaling happens in a spreadsheet
• News and events happen across 3 more tabs

Result: Constant context switching. No single source of truth. Manual copy-pasting between systems.

Key Points (for non-traders)

The analogy: Imagine running a business where your email, calendar, documents, accounting, and project management are all on different platforms that do not talk to each other. No Slack. No Google Workspace. No Notion. Just 6 separate apps with no integration. That is what trading looks like today.
Why it is painful: Every time a trader wants to act on an idea, they must switch apps 3-5 times, manually copy data between them, and risk missing time-sensitive information while switching.
Why it has not been solved: The tools that exist are each excellent at their one job (TradingView is the best at charts, MT5 is reliable for execution). But nobody has built the layer that connects them into a workflow.
Visual Layout: Top half: headline in large bold text. Bottom half: a simple diagram showing 6 boxes arranged in a scattered pattern. Each box has an icon and label (Chart icon + "Analysis", Terminal icon + "Execution", Chat icon + "Communication", Bank icon + "Account", Book icon + "Journaling", News icon + "Events"). Dashed red lines between boxes with X marks showing disconnection. Center text: "No unified system." Clean, not cluttered. A non-trader should understand it in 3 seconds.
Speaker Notes: "Think of it like this: imagine if every time you wanted to send an email, you had to open a separate app to check your calendar, another to find the contact, another to draft the email, and another to send it. That is what trading workflows look like right now. Six completely separate tools, zero integration."
Investor takeaway: "I now understand the problem even though I don't trade. The workflow is fragmented across 6 systems with no connection."

Why the Current Workflow Is Broken

Purpose

Show the actual daily workflow a trader goes through — step by step — to complete one single trade. Make the investor feel the pain of how many handoffs, app switches, and manual steps are involved. This converts the abstract "fragmentation" concept into a concrete, visceral sequence.

Suggested Copy

One trade. Seven steps. Six different apps.
When users chain these products together to complete a single task, the reality is painful.

The 7-Step Workflow (show as a horizontal flow or numbered list)

Step 1 — Check the News: Open ForexFactory or Twitter. Scan headlines for market-moving events. Switch to another news site for a second opinion. (2 apps opened)
Step 2 — Analyze the Chart: Open TradingView. Find the instrument. Apply indicators. Identify a potential trade setup. Take a screenshot. (3 apps opened)
Step 3 — Check the Community: Open Discord or Telegram. Scroll through noisy channels. Find what the mentor or group thinks. See if anyone else sees the same setup. (4 apps opened)
Step 4 — Decide and Execute: Switch to MT5 or TradeLocker. Manually enter the instrument, lot size, stop loss, and take profit. Copy numbers from TradingView screenshot. Hope you did not make a typo. (5 apps opened)
Step 5 — Monitor the Trade: Toggle between TradingView (to watch the chart), MT5 (to see P&L), and Discord (for group updates). Constant tab switching. (Still 5 apps)
Step 6 — Journal the Result: After the trade closes, open TradeZella or a spreadsheet. Manually enter the trade details, screenshots, and lessons learned. (6 apps opened)
Step 7 — Manage the Account: Need to check margin? Deposit? Withdraw profits? Open the broker's separate website. Another login, another interface. (7 touchpoints total)

The Four Consequences

Constant Context Switching: Too many tabs and apps. Users lose time and focus toggling between tracking, chatting, and trading environments.
Manual Handoffs: Copy-pasting entries, stop-losses, and updates between apps is slow and introduces operational risk and errors.
No Single Source of Truth: No unified history or central place for decisions, actions, and follow-up. Everything is scattered across 6 platforms.
Broken Team Operations: Onboarding new community members requires teaching them 6 separate tools. Communities cannot operate cleanly as a unit.
Visual Layout: Top: headline. Middle: horizontal flow diagram with 7 numbered circles connected by arrows. Each circle has a tiny icon of the app used and a 2-word label. Below the flow: red pain markers at each transition point (the arrows between steps) with mini-labels like "manual copy," "context switch," "no connection." Below the flow: the 4 consequence cards in a 2x2 grid. Bottom right: one bold line: "The problem is not the individual tools. The problem is the fragmented system."
Speaker Notes: "This is one trade. Not a complex strategy. Just one basic trade from start to finish requires touching 6 different applications and performing at least 3 manual copy-paste operations. Multiply this by 5-20 trades per day, 5 days a week, 52 weeks a year. The cumulative friction is enormous. And every manual handoff is a point where errors, missed information, or delayed execution can cost real money."
Investor takeaway: "The problem is not one bad tool. It is a broken system. One trade requires 7 steps across 6 apps. That is operationally insane."
