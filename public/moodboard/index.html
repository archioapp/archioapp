The page should breathe with the story it tells.

As the reader scrolls through the problem, the world behind the words grows hostile: dry earth, sandstorm, lightning. As they scroll into the solution, the world settles: dawn, garden, water, stillness. One continuous shot. One emotional arc. No cuts.

Problem · 01

The room is empty.

Discord goes quiet at midnight. The mentor is asleep. The market is not. You are alone with a chart and a decision you don’t yet know how to make.

Ecosystem cue · dry earth, no wind, no sound. The stillness of absence.

Problem · 02

Too many voices. Not one signal.

Ten Discords. Three newsletters. Fourteen screenshots of someone else’s P&L. None of it teaches you anything that will hold, because none of it is audited, ordered, or yours.

Ecosystem cue · wind rises, the horizon disappears, sand drags across the frame.

And then the market shows up.

It doesn’t ask if you’re ready. It doesn’t care who promised what. Without a system you can cite — a rule, a record, a reason — every setup becomes a gamble dressed as a plan.

The room remembers.

Every trade, every rule, every lesson tagged to the moment it was learned — stored in a structure that the market can’t erase and a Discord can’t drown out.

Director notes · the spec & the pitch

This page is the argument, not the artifact.

Everything above this line is a concept prototype. It runs in the browser with zero assets — no video, no GPU shaders, no external libraries. The ecosystem is a particle system and three gradient layers driven by one scalar: scroll progress, zero to one. What follows is how to pitch it, what to build, and why the effect works on the reader without them noticing.

The idea in one sentence

Make the background feel the story.

The reader does not consciously see the weather change. They feel the story is true. The harsh sections read as harsher; the calm sections read as calmer. The copy does less work because the atmosphere does half of it.

Why it works psychologically

Environment primes emotion.

Readers process ambient visual state before they process words. A chaotic background primes threat; a settled background primes safety. By the time the eye reaches the sentence, the body has already agreed with it. This is the difference between a pitch that persuades and a pitch that convinces.

Why not video

Video is heavy, rigid, and skippable.

  • Weight. A 20-second 4K loop is 30-80 MB. First paint dies.
  • Rigidity. Video plays on its own timeline; scroll doesn’t control it naturally.
  • Skip. Phones pause autoplay video when the tab is out of focus.
  • Control. Canvas lets us respond to scroll, cursor, and section enter/exit.
What XCLAY actually builds

One canvas, six scenes, one scroll driver.

  • Canvas — fixed to the viewport, full bleed, z-index 0.
  • Particle pool — ~800 recycled particles, typed (sand, rain, leaf, droplet, dust).
  • Sky gradient — interpolated between scene stops every frame.
  • Lightning & sun overlays — triggered by scene, faded by progress.
  • Copy layer — regular DOM with glass cards, z-index 2.
  • Reduced-motion fallback — static gradient, no canvas.
Performance envelope

Under 60 fps on a 2019 MacBook Air, under 45 fps on mid-tier Android.

  • Cap particles — 800 desktop, 300 mobile.
  • Single rAF loop — never more than one draw per frame.
  • DPR-aware canvas — render at devicePixelRatio, clamped to 2.
  • Pause off-screen — stop the loop when document.hidden.
  • No box-shadow on particles — shadow-blur is the #1 performance killer.
Accessibility

The ecosystem must be skippable.

  • prefers-reduced-motion — disable canvas entirely, serve a static vertical gradient.
  • Contrast — copy cards carry a glass background, never sit directly on particles.
  • Aria-hidden — the canvas is purely decorative; screen readers skip it.
  • Keyboard — no hover-only behaviour. Everything the canvas does, scroll drives.
