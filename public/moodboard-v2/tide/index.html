Director notes · for Xclay

An elemental lens. Nothing else to learn.

The Tide is water only. No character, no room, no metaphor that needs translation. The reader’s body already knows what it means when a storm settles into a still pond and a single drop falls.

The idea, in one sentence

One element. Five amplitudes. A single final drop.

A canvas water surface driven by a sum of sine waves. Amplitude, frequency, and foam density are all scroll-bound. Ripples are spawned on top as concentric circles. The final droplet is an authored event that triggers near the end of the scroll range.

Why it works

Water is pre-installed in the nervous system.

Every human has watched water change. No one has to explain what a calm surface means or what a storm feels like. The Tide skips the entire cognitive layer and speaks directly to the body — which is exactly the layer Archio is trying to calm.

Why not video

Stock footage of water is famously wrong.

Every seascape video has color-graded sunsets, seagulls, wind sound. We need a painted water surface that scrubs with scroll, without audio, without characters, without a horizon line that locks the frame. An authored canvas gives exactly that.

What Xclay builds

One canvas. Two layers. One event.

Layer 1: water surface, rendered as a filled path updated per frame using four sine waves with scroll-driven amplitudes and frequencies. Layer 2: FX — foam particles at crests, expanding ripples, the authored final drop. Sky behind is a CSS gradient tied to scroll. Target: 60 fps on M-class, 30 fps on mid-range Android.
