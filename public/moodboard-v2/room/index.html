Archio · Lens I

The light in the room is the story.

The hero doesn’t move. The chair doesn’t move. The coffee mug doesn’t move. Only the sun moves — from the cold fluorescent of a four-a.m. panic to the amber of the turn to the stillness of a single candle at close.

Scroll slowly. Watch the window.

Scene 04 : 12 A.M. Fluorescent · cold white
Act I · Midnight panic

Every trader knows this room.

It’s late. The overhead light is too bright. The screen is too loud. There’s a half-drunk coffee on the desk and a position that has moved the wrong way since dinner.

This is where most traders meet their worst decisions. This is the room Archio is designed to pull you out of.

Emotional register Panic Cold blue · hard shadow
Act II · Blue hour

The first light outside is still cold.

The fluorescent fades. A thin blue bleed starts at the horizon. The room is quieter but not yet warmer. This is the moment most traders miss — the space between last night’s loss and today’s open.

It’s the first moment Archio asks you a different question than your PnL does.

Scene 05 : 48 A.M. Ambient blue · long shadow
Act III · The turn

Then one thing changes. The horizon warms.

Amber at the sill. The beam angles across the desk. Dust catches in the light — for the first time you can see the air. The room has been the same room the whole time. Only the angle of the sun was different.

This is the hinge of the page. Everything before this was the problem. Everything after this is the product.

07 : 04 A.M. · The amber horizon · the hinge
Act IV · Noon clarity

The light is even. The shadow is short.

The sun is overhead. The room is fully visible. The monitor’s glow is competing with nothing — because nothing in the room is trying to hide. Every chart is legible. Every rule is on the table.

This is the one-brain state. Calm, cool, complete.

Scene 12 : 30 P.M. Even light · minimal shadow
Act V · Golden hour

The room is honest in this light.

Amber floods in from the right. Long shadows stretch back across the desk. The coffee mug catches it on one side. This is the hour when a trader reviews what they did today — and Archio shows them, with citations, why it worked.

Every decision is marked. Every rule earned its place.

Scene 18 : 22 P.M. Warm amber · long shadow
Act VI · Stillness

A single candle. The day is closed.

The window is dark. The overhead is off. The monitor is off. One warm point on the desk. The trader sits back. Nothing left to do tonight.

This is what we’re actually selling. Not features. Not charts. This moment.

23 : 48 P.M. · Candle only · the held breath
Director notes · for Xclay

What this lens is. What it is not.

The Room is the cinematography lens. The story is told by a single composition whose lighting changes as the reader scrolls. This document is both the mood board and the build spec.

The idea, in one sentence

Same scene. Changing light. The reader feels the turn.

One fixed composition of a trading desk by a window. The only thing that changes with scroll is the quality of the light — color temperature, shadow angle, window glow, lamp glow, dust density. Six keyframes tied to scroll percent; everything between is interpolated.

Why it works

Cinematography bypasses language.

Traders don’t need to read the words to feel the difference between 4 a.m. and golden hour. Their nervous system knows. The copy just names what their body already sensed. This is the most felt direction of the four.

Why not video

Video is heavy, rigid, and can’t hold on scroll.

A 4K MP4 is 20–40 MB and can’t perfectly sync to scroll position across devices. This prototype is ~40 KB of SVG + CSS + a tiny canvas. It runs at 60 fps on every device. It scrubs with the scroll bar. It is the same motion, authored, not recorded.

What Xclay builds

One SVG scene, six light states, one interpolator.

Scene is authored in SVG (vector, sharp on retina). Six moments define sky gradient, interior tint, beam angle, lamp/candle/fluor intensity, dust density. Scroll progress picks the two nearest moments and lerps every channel. Dust motes are a particle pool on a small canvas (~200 particles max).
