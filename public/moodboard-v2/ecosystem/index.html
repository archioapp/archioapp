Lens 04 · The Ecosystem

A world that moves with the story it tells.

Scroll from desert into garden. What you feel is what the product does.

Act I · Desert

The trader's desk. No weather. Just heat.

Charts screamed at from every direction. Every setup looks the same. Every mistake feels novel. Nothing is remembered. Nothing is connected. A cracked ground of a thousand undocumented decisions.

Act II · Sandstorm

Then the noise doubles.

More data. More tools. More opinions. Each one a grain of sand. The horizon dissolves into the texture of everything arriving at once and nothing staying.

Act III · The Storm

Panic and premonition strike the same place.

Lightning is recognition the body has before the mind does — you knew this trade was wrong, and you took it anyway. The rain is every edge, every rule, every scar the trader carries.

Then one thing changes.

The room begins to remember.

Act IV · Dawn

The first warm light.

Cortex cites. Rule Forge answers. For the first time in a thousand trades, a decision is met with a sentence that remembers the last one like it.

Act V · Garden

What was cracked is now rooted.

Mentor signatures root. Network intelligence grows. What the trader could never see alone now takes shape as a living record of how they win and how they lose.

Act VI · Water

Flow replaces friction.

Every decision finds its precedent. Every trade feeds the ledger that feeds the next trade. The hand steadies. The system carries the weight the trader used to carry alone.

Act VII · Stillness

Nothing moves. Everything is held.

The weather ends. The world the reader scrolled through is now the world the product delivers.

Director Notes · Lens 04

The Ecosystem — the world shifts with the story.

The most emotional of the four lenses. The background is a living environment that moves through seven weather states as the reader scrolls. The reader doesn’t just read about the problem and the solution; their body feels the difference between the two. Desert discomfort, storm dread, dawn relief, garden ease, water flow, stillness as the ending.

Why it works

Humans process weather pre-linguistically. Before they read the copy, their nervous system has already registered "unsafe → safe." The page sells Archio at an emotional level while also selling it at a logical one.

Build

Layered CSS gradients for the sky, ground, and orb — no images. One 2D canvas for particles (sand, rain, leaves, droplets). Lightning is a DOM overlay with a timed opacity pulse. Scene transitions are pure CSS via a data-scene attribute on <body>.

Performance

60 fps, ~240 particles desktop, 140 mobile. Gradient layers GPU-composited. No WebGL. Total weight budget: < 24 kB for the entire scene system.

Psychology

Desert (discomfort) → storm (dread) → dawn (relief) → garden (reward) → water (flow) → stillness (mastery). The arc mirrors the trader's own transformation, performed by the page underneath them.

Accessibility

All animation respects prefers-reduced-motion — scene colors still shift, but particles freeze and lightning is disabled. Canvas is decorative (aria-hidden). Full narrative lives in semantic HTML.

Extension

Each scene can bind to a product surface. Desert = Cortex not invoked. Garden = a verdict cited. Water = mentor signature rooted. The scene system becomes a design language for the entire product, not just the landing page.

Scene blueprint

Seven weather states. Each one owns a sky gradient, a ground gradient, an orb state, and a particle program. Transitions are 2 seconds with a flow-ease curve — slow enough to feel, fast enough not to bore.
